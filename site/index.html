amanu

A meeting recorder for the Mac. Afterwards the recording, the transcript and the summary are in a folder.

Download for macOS

Free, open source, signed and notarised. macOS 15 or later, Apple Silicon or Intel.

You don’t start it

amanu starts recording when a call app takes the microphone, and stops when the call lets it go. Zoom, Teams, Meet in a browser tab, Slack huddles, Telegram, FaceTime — no per-app integration and nothing to click.

Only apps on a list count, because everything else that opens the microphone — dictation, Voice Memos, a browser tab checking levels — would otherwise become a recording of whatever was in the room. A missed meeting costs one click. A false one costs privacy.

Stopping is the part that has to be right

Three rules end a session: nobody on the mic and quiet on the far end, silence on both tracks, and a hard ceiling on length. The failure mode of getting this wrong is a fifteen-hour recording made overnight — which is a thing that has happened, to the program amanu descends from.

It says what it is thinking

“ready”, “Zoom on the mic for 7s”, “quiet for 40s”. Which is the difference between debugging a missed recording and guessing at it. One checkbox turns the whole thing off.

A call that lasted a moment isn’t a meeting

An automatic recording shorter than forty-five seconds is deleted rather than transcribed. Recordings you started yourself are never auto-stopped and never discarded: if you pressed the button, only you decide.

Only the call is on the far-end track

amanu taps the call app’s output, not everything the Mac plays, so notification dings and the video you opened afterwards stay out of the transcript — and “the far end went quiet” means the call ended.

It transcribes on the machine

Local by default: NVIDIA’s parakeet, 25 European languages including Russian, running on the Neural Engine after the meeting at roughly twenty seconds per hour of audio. Nothing leaves the Mac.

Hand it an AssemblyAI or OpenAI key and it will use that instead — better on Russian, and it tells apart several people sharing one side of the call. That is the one part that isn’t local, and amanu says so out loud when the engine is chosen rather than in a paragraph nobody reads.

Recording writes two tracks on purpose — your microphone and the far end, separately. Speech models do better on clean single-source audio, and mic-versus-system is two-party diarisation for free.

One folder per meeting

Under ~/Recordings, named so that a folder listing is readable on its own: the time first, then the calendar’s title for the meeting, then the app that was holding the microphone.

2026.08.17-1400 Integration sync (zoom.us)/
transcript.md the transcript, laid out for reading, with names where they are known
summary.md topic, key points, decisions, action items, open questions
transcript.json the canonical transcript: which engine, and timed, speaker-tagged segments
speakers.json who each label is, where the name came from, and the line that proves it
meta.json times, duration, what triggered it, why it stopped, the meeting’s title and attendees
audio.m4a kept only if you ask: your mic on the left channel, the far end on the right

The audio is deleted once the transcript exists, unless you turn that off. A failed transcription always keeps it.

It works out who was talking

A transcript comes out of a recogniser saying me and them A. Before the summary is written, amanu puts real people to those labels — from the calendar’s attendee list, and from the meeting itself, because people say each other’s names out loud.

Two rules stand between a model’s answer and the file. Only high confidence is applied: guessing from who was invited doesn’t qualify. And the quote the answer came from has to exist — it is checked against the transcript, because a model that invents a name usually invents its source too. A label nobody could identify stays them A, which is honest. A wrong name is a transcript that lies.

And writes down what was decided

Topic, key points, decisions, action items, open questions — from the claude or codex CLI already signed in on your Mac, from an API key, or from ollama with no network at all.

The subscription comes before the metered key, and a spent allowance is not an error: the next backend takes over. Naming and summarising both need a model, which a laptop on a train hasn’t got — so neither fails. The session is marked as owing the work and picked up later. A meeting recorded on a plane still gets its summary that evening.

What’s recorded, and what’s still owed on it

Every session, newest first, with a column each for the transcript, the names and the summary, and what state each of them is in. Select one and it shows how the meeting opened and, per speaker, their first line and their longest — the two lines that identify a person when nobody said any names — with a field to type the name yourself. A name typed there is never overwritten by a later run.

Built for the ways a recording is actually lost

A crash mid-meeting costs nothing

Recording writes uncompressed audio, which has no packet table and no finalising step: whatever reached the disk plays. Compressed audio killed mid-file is undecodable to the last byte — measured here, 99 KB written and zero seconds recoverable. Compression happens afterwards, once the transcript exists.

An interrupted session is adopted

A live recording keeps a small manifest beside it. At the next launch amanu finds every one whose owner is gone, writes the file a clean stop would have written, and puts the session through the ordinary queue.

A track that stops growing says so

Both tracks are watched while recording: a stall is a notification then and there, and a line in the session’s metadata afterwards. A one-sided transcript should be explainable, not a mystery.

The microphone is followed, not inherited

Headphones connecting, AirPods coming out of an ear, the call app taking the input device — each rebuild is recorded with how much silence it cost. Switch microphones mid-meeting and the track moves with you.

Three places to see whether it is recording

A window, a Dock icon that turns red with the elapsed time as its badge, and the feather in the menu bar. All three show one state, and any of them can be switched off.

Three, because a menu bar item is not a dependable place for the only control of a recorder. When the menu bar runs out of room macOS parks the item off-screen — measured at x = −9406 on a laptop with a notch — and a menu bar manager can hide it outright. It stays perfectly clickable and completely invisible, which for a program whose whole job is answering “am I recording?” is the worst way it can fail.

In English or in Russian, following whichever your Mac is set to.

Get amanu

Download for macOS

Drag Amanu.app to Applications and open it. The disk image is signed with a Developer ID certificate and carries a stapled notarisation ticket, so Gatekeeper opens it without argument. Afterwards it updates itself, and an update that arrives during a meeting waits for the meeting to end.
